Financial and Strategic Analysis of the Company

Financial and Strategic Analysis of the Company

Introduction

The course Financial and Strategic Analysis of the Company is part of the first skills block, Designing the financial and non-financial strategy of organisations, of the Expert en Stratégie Financière (RNCP40177) title (Expert in Financial Strategy), recognised by France Compétences.

It is offered to students of the INSEEC Master of Science programmes Finance d’Entreprise & Ingénierie Financière (Corporate Finance & Financial Engineering), Manager Financier, Banque & Assurance (Financial Manager, Banking & Insurance), Banque d’Affaires & Ingénierie Financière (Investment Banking & Financial Engineering) and Analyse Financière Internationale (International Financial Analysis). The module aims to develop in-depth expertise in financial diagnosis, performance analysis and the design of financial and non-financial strategies, in line with international standards (IFRS, GAAP), ESG issues and regulatory requirements (CSRD, AMF, etc.).

The course builds on the operational methodology of the Banque de France, as practised by its financial analysts in the rating of companies. This methodology bases the analysis on the review of consolidated accounts and relies on a 6-step process applied to more than 5,000 groups every year.

The 6-step methodology

Step Content Quests
1 Environment, strategy and organisation — SWOT, legal, economic and financial analysis Q5
2 Critical review of the information — consolidation scope, methods (full / proportionate consolidation / equity method), audit certification Q2
3 Analysis of performance — recurring net income (RNR ≈ ¾ × RCAI, i.e. pre-tax ordinary income), trends, sector comparison Q4
4 Financial structure — FPNR (restated equity), goodwill assessment, restated debt, financial autonomy (CAF, i.e. cash flow) Q3, Q4
5 Liquidity — static analysis (FRNG, net working capital / BFR, working-capital requirement), dynamic (FTA/CA), forward-looking (FTD over N+1, N+2), financial risks Q3, Q8
6 Rating decision — financial rating, qualitative profile, outlook, recommendation for subsidiaries Q3, Q6

Learning Objectives

  • Diagnosis based on individual accounts and on consolidated accounts.
  • Study of profitability, solvency, financial structure and return (ROCE, ROE).
  • Analysis of listed and unlisted companies.

Targeted RNCP knowledge and skills

  1. Carry out economic, regulatory, legal and tax monitoring (Finance Bill, CSRD directive, IFRS standards, US GAAP, AMF, etc.) in order to build a financial strategy that will support the organisation’s activity and development in compliance with the national and international standards in force in its sector and on its market.
  2. Analyse the organisation’s business model, its CSR strategy and its market in order to identify strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and risks, and to co-build its strategy and business plan with management.
  3. Lead the production of financial statements (balance sheet, income statement and cash-flow statement) while ensuring data quality and compliance with standards (IFRS, GAAP, etc.) in order to produce reliable documents that can be shared with internal and external stakeholders and used as a basis for decision-making.
  4. Lead the production of non-financial performance statements while ensuring the quality of the data included in the ESG criteria in order to produce reports that present the CSR strategy to internal and external stakeholders.
  5. Analyse the organisation’s financial data from the statements produced previously using relevant ratios (liquidity, solvency and profitability ratios, BFR (working-capital requirement), etc.) and synthesise the data in order to assess its financial soundness, solvency and performance.
  6. Design the company’s financial strategy (medium- and long-term financial planning, budgets, cash plan) according to the company’s strategic choices and CSR policy, in order to agree common objectives with senior management, identify the means to achieve them and secure the company’s activity.
  7. Translate the company’s CSR strategy into ESG criteria and lead the implementation of their measurement in order to support the company’s CSR policy, measure it and produce the non-financial performance statements.
  8. Design and run the budgeting process in coordination with the organisation’s various functions and with management control, reflecting the defined strategy in order to achieve the financial objectives and ensure its financial performance.

Sessions

Centred on an active learning approach, the course turns students into players1, modules into quests, assessments into experience points (XP), and skills into levels to be reached.

Session plan

The course runs over 3 days of 6 hours of teaching (360 minutes each, 1,080 minutes, i.e. 18 h in total), divided into 3 main quests. Breaks are not counted. The minute-by-minute detail is in the planning.

Each quest begins with 20 to 30 minutes of live theory (included in its duration); the reference pages serve as pre-reading (at most 45 minutes per evening).

Exams. The 1 h and 3 h 30 exams are outside the 18 h: they take place in a separate session. During the three days, only the “boss” cases and XP assess the students.

Reference pages: Banque de France method, financial statements, performance, financial structure, liquidity, rating, formulas and glossary.

Game theme: “The Finance Strategists”

Students play junior financial analysts in a fictional firm called “StratFin Corp”. Their mission: climb the ranks to become Experts in Financial Strategy, by completing quests, collaborating, and producing strategic deliverables.

Structure of the 3 days (18 h)

Each day is divided into 3 main quests, with team activities, end-of-day bosses (practical cases) and XP awarded for each activity.

Day 1: Exploring the financial statements (360 min)

Objective: players discover the basics of financial diagnosis and the foundations of the Banque de France methodology, a prerequisite for analysing consolidated accounts.

  • Prerequisites: notions of general accounting (balance sheet, income statement).
  • Pre-reading (≈ 15 min): The three financial statements.
  • Blocks: kick-off (15), Q1 (110), Q2 (100), Q3 + boss (135).
  • End-of-day deliverable: oral presentation of the diagnosis of MEDITECH SAS and a credit recommendation (10 min per presentation).
  • XP: Q1 +100, Q2 +150, Q3 +200.

Day 2: Performance and strategy analysis (360 min)

Objective: players learn to assess a company’s performance and strategy.

  • Prerequisites: having attended Day 1 (or having read the reference pages performance and formulas).
  • Evening preparation (≈ 45 min):
    • read the performance page and the sheet of main ratios (formulas);
    • collect, from the annual report chosen on Day 1, the data needed to compute equity, EBIT, financial debt and BFR (working-capital requirement);
    • compute at least 2 ratios and note their interpretation.
  • Blocks: Q4 (120), Q5 (105), Q6 + boss (135).
  • End-of-day deliverable: comparative study of a listed company (CAC 40) and an unlisted SME in the same sector, presented in 5 min per team.
  • XP: Q4 +150, Q5 +150, Q6 +200.

Day 3: Monitoring, ESG and financial strategy (360 min)

Objective: players become strategists able to integrate ESG and regulatory issues.

  • Prerequisites: having attended Day 2; the annual report chosen on Day 1 is reused.
  • Evening preparation (≈ 45 min):
    • read the Déclaration de Performance Extra-Financière (DPEF, non-financial performance statement) or the CSR section of the annual report;
    • complete with a short news article (green bonds, carbon tax or sustainable finance);
    • identify a CSR/ESG initiative and its possible financial impact (costs, image, funding, profitability), in at most 5 slides or one page.
  • Blocks: Q7 (100), Q8 (110), Q9 + final boss (140), closing (10).
  • End-of-day deliverable: defence of a strategic report integrating ESG and finance; the “at home” part of Q9 (cost-of-capital adjustment, 1.5-page report) completes the quest grade.
  • XP: Q7 +150, Q8 +150, Q9 +250.

Module assessment

In this course, students move through a gamified learning universe. Every activity, whether an individual mission, group work or a presentation, earns experience points (XP). These XP reflect engagement, the quality of the work and progress in acquiring skills. The system is structured in levels: Junior Analyst, Confirmed Analyst, Financial Strategist, and finally Experts in Financial Strategy.

As they accumulate XP, students unlock badges recognising their technical skills (diagnosis, ratios, ESG), their collaborative spirit and their strategic ability. This system fosters motivation, active participation and gradual skill development, while making learning more immersive and stimulating.

The XP per quest, the levels, the badges and the calculation of the final grade (40% XP, 60% exam) are detailed on the Assessment page. The 1 h and 3 h 30 exams are outside the 18 h.
